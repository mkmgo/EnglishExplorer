Grammar Guide: Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

Both tenses talk about things that happened before now, but the main difference is how we connect them to the present moment:

1. Simple Past

  • When to use: We use the simple past for actions that happened at a specific time in the past. The time period is finished (for example: yesterday, last week, in 2010).
  • Focus: History, finished facts, or a specific moment that has no direct connection to right now.
  • Example: I visited London in 2022. (The trip is finished, and we are focusing on the past date).

2. Present Perfect (have/has + past participle)

  • When to use: We use the present perfect for actions that happened in the past, but they are connected to the present. We use it for:
  • Life experiences (without saying exactly when).
  • Recent actions that have a result right now.
  • Unfinished time periods (like today or this week).
  • Focus: The present result or current state.
  • Example: I have visited London three times. (This is part of my life experience up to today).

Exercises

Exercise 1: Choose the correct option

Choose either the Simple Past or the Present Perfect to complete each sentence.

  1. I lost my keys yesterday, so I could not get into my house.
  2. She has lived in Manchester for five years, and she still lives there now.
  3. We went to Spain for our holidays last summer.
  4. Have you ever eaten sushi in your life?
  5. He has just finished his homework, so he can go outside to play now.
  6. My father worked as a teacher from 2005 to 2015.
  7. I can't find my phone. I think I have lost it!
  8. They have watched that film twice this week.

Exercise 2: Fill in the blanks

  1. My sister bought a new bicycle last weekend.
  2. Look! Somebody has broken the kitchen window.
  3. I haven't seen my best friend since last Monday.
  4. Shakespeare wrote many famous plays.
  5. We already have eaten lunch, so we are not hungry.
