Signal Words

Here is a new, basic exercise focused on common time words (signal words) that help you choose between the Present Perfect and the Simple Past.

Exercise: Signal Words Practice

Look at the word in brackets in each sentence. Choose whether to use the Simple Past or the Present Perfect.

Questions

  1. I have not finished my lunch yet.
  2. We went to the cinema last night.
  3. Have you ever been to Scotland?
  4. She has just bought a new car. It is parked outside.
  5. They lived in that house ten years ago.
  6. I have known my best friend since 2015.
  7. He has worked here for five years.
  8. Did you see the football match yesterday ?

Time words

  1. yet -> Present Perfect (I have not finished my lunch yet.) – "Yet" is used in negative sentences and questions for unfinished time up to now.
  2. last night -> Simple Past (We went to the cinema last night.) – "Last night" is a specific, finished point in the past.
  3. ever -> Present Perfect (Have you ever been to Scotland?) – "Ever" is used in questions to ask about life experience.
  4. just -> Present Perfect (She has just bought a new car.) – "Just" is used for very recent actions with a present result.
  5. ago -> Simple Past (They lived in that house ten years ago.) – "Ago" counts backward from the present to a finished point in the past.
  6. since -> Present Perfect (I have known my best friend since 2015.) – "Since" points to the exact starting time of an action that continues now.
  7. for -> Present Perfect (He has worked here for five years.) – "For" measures the duration of time up to the present.
  8. yesterday -> Simple Past (Did you see the football match yesterday?) – "Yesterday" points to a specific finished day.
