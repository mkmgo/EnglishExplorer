Luck

The Tale of the Unluckiest Girl: The Opening 10 Minutes

Sam Greenfield is eighteen years old, but she has a big secret: she is the unluckiest person in the whole world. Every single day, simple things go completely wrong for her.

In the morning, her toaster always burns her bread. When she tries to open a door, the handle falls off in her hand. If she holds something soft, like a glass of juice or a bag of flour, she drops it onto the floor. Bad luck follows her wherever she goes!

Today is a very big day for Sam. Because she has turned eighteen, she must leave her foster home. A foster home is a place where children live when they do not have a family to care for them.

Before Sam packs her bags, she goes to find her young friend, Hazel. Hazel is a sweet little girl who still lives at the foster home. Hazel is looking for a lucky charm—a special item that she believes will help her find a forever family. Sam loves Hazel very much and promises to help her if she can.

Sam picks up her boxes and moves into her very first flat. But as soon as she steps inside, her terrible luck starts again!

  • The front door key gets stuck fast in the lock.
  • Her heavy wooden furniture breaks as she tries to set it up.
  • She trips over her own bags and drops her clothes on the floor.

Sam sits on her bed and sighs. Moving out is hard, especially when everything goes wrong!

By evening, Sam feels tired, hungry, and a bit sad. She goes outside and sits down on the cold pavement near the street to eat her simple dinner.

Suddenly, a quiet black cat walks up to her. Instead of chasing the cat away, Sam smiles softly. She breaks off a small piece of her food and gently offers it to the animal. The black cat eats the food, looks up at Sam with bright eyes, and then quietly walks away into the dark night.

Grammar Exercises: Pronouns & Past Simple

Circle or write the correct word in brackets to complete each sentence.

  1. Sam is very unlucky, so bad things happen to (she / her) every day.
  2. Hazel lives in the foster home because (she / it) is still young.
  3. Sam moved into a new flat, but (it / they) had broken doors.
  4. The black cat ate the food after Sam gave (him / it) a piece.
  5. "Will (you / your) help me find a lucky charm?" asked Hazel.
