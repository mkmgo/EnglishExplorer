Lisa's New School

On Monday morning, Lisa woke up early because she felt both excited and a little nervous. It was her first day at her new school, and although she had practised her English every week for months, she still worried that she might forget the right words. Lisa only recently moved here from Germany.

After breakfast, her mother drove her to school. When they arrived, Lisa saw hundreds of students walking through the gates, laughing and chatting in groups. She took a deep breath and told herself that everything would be fine.

In the classroom, a girl with a friendly smile turned around and said, "Hi, I'm Sophie. What's your name?" Lisa answered quietly, "I'm Lisa. Nice to meet you." Sophie asked if Lisa would like to sit next to her, and Lisa happily agreed.

At lunchtime, they walked to the cafeteria together. Lisa wasn't sure what to choose, so Sophie helped her read the menu. "If you like chicken, you should try this one," Sophie suggested, pointing at a tray. Lisa smiled and said she would.

While they were eating, Sophie asked Lisa about her hobbies. Lisa explained that she loved tennis and reading, especially short stories. Sophie's eyes lit up — she liked the same books! They talked for the rest of the lunch, discovering that they had more in common than Lisa had expected.

By the end of the day, Lisa felt tired but proud. She had spoken English all day, made a new friend, and survived her first lunch in the cafeteria. As she walked home, she thought about how nervous she had felt that morning — and how much better she felt now.

That evening, she wrote in her journal: "Today was hard, but I did it. Tomorrow, I will try to speak even more."

Structures:

  • Past perfect — "she had practised", "more than she had expected" — quietly modelling that an earlier action can sit behind a past-simple story
  • Past continuous — "while they were eating" — background action alongside a main event
  • First-conditional-style advice — "If you like chicken, you should try this one" — a natural, low-pressure exposure to conditional advice
  • Reported/indirect speech — "asked if Lisa would like to...", "explained that she loved..." — the shift from direct to indirect question and statement
  • Modals of possibility and advice — might, would, should, will
  • Connectors — because, although, so, while, when, especially
